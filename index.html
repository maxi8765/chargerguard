A free alarm for the charger you leave behind

People keep leaving their chargers behind. If your charger has a spare USB port, a small Bluetooth beacon plugged into it lets your Android phone warn you when you walk away without it. The app is free to use, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy from me.

How it works

The beacon is a USB stick about the size of a fingernail. It broadcasts a Bluetooth signal about ten times a second. Charger Guard listens for it, and when it stops hearing it after you unplug, the alarm goes off.

1. Plug the beacon into your charger

Use the spare USB-A port on your wall charger, laptop or power board. The port needs to stay powered when your phone isn't plugged in.

2. Charge as normal

When you plug the phone in, Charger Guard looks for the beacon. Once it hears it, the guard is armed. You don't need to open the app.

3. Walk away without it, and it rings

After you unplug, if the beacon is out of range for 5 seconds (you can set 2 to 30), the phone sounds an alarm-clock style alert, even on silent or Do Not Disturb if you allow it.

What you need

  • An Android phone running Android 8 or later. There's no iPhone version.
  • A charger with a spare USB-A port that stays powered. Most dual-port wall chargers and power boards with USB work.
  • One Feasycom FSC-BP101E USB beacon. It's the one this app was built and tested with.

Setup takes about two minutes

  1. Download the APK on your phone and open it. Android will ask you to allow installs from your browser or file manager; allow it for this install.
  2. Open Charger Guard. The setup wizard asks for Nearby devices, precise location and notifications. Android requires location access for Bluetooth scanning.
  3. Allow alarm access: alarm-clock style alert, Do Not Disturb access, alarm volume up, and unrestricted battery use.
  4. Plug the beacon into your charger, hold the phone next to it and tap Find beacon. Pick the entry marked Feasycom.
  5. Play the test alarm, then plug your phone in.

Other features

Quiet at home

Save your home Wi-Fi and Charger Guard switches off completely there: no alarm and no Bluetooth scanning.

Battery use

It only scans while charging, and after you unplug until you walk away. Never when idle or at home.

You set the delay

Choose how long the beacon must be out of range before the alarm sounds, from 2 to 30 seconds.

Privacy

Charger Guard has no account and stores everything on your phone. It never reads GPS coordinates or keeps a location history. Android labels Bluetooth scanning and reading your Wi-Fi name as location access, which is why it asks.

  • Each time you open the app, it sends one anonymous "app opened" count to the developer, with no device ID, location or personal details. It never retries or reports errors.
  • The beacon address and your home Wi-Fi name are saved only on your phone.
  • It never pairs with or connects to the beacon. It only listens.
  • This website counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which uses no cookies.

Questions

What if I unplug the charger from the wall first?

The beacon loses power and disappears, so the alarm will go off. Unplug your phone first, or keep the phone near you and tap Stop.

It stopped working after a while. Why?

Some phones, especially OPPO, Xiaomi, Huawei and some Samsung models, shut down background apps to save battery. Allow unrestricted battery use, and on those phones also allow background activity and auto-launch in Charger Guard's app settings. Phone software updates can reset these settings, so check them again after an update.

Will other beacons work?

Any Bluetooth LE beacon that broadcasts from a fixed address should work, but only the FSC-BP101E has been tested.

Why isn't it on Google Play?

It’s a free download straight from this page rather than through an app store, which is why Android asks you to allow the install.

Is there an iPhone version?

No. iPhones don't let apps keep listening for Bluetooth beacons in the background in the same way.

How far away does the alarm go off?

It depends on walls, your phone and the delay you choose. In our indoor test it went off at about 25 m with the 5-second delay.
